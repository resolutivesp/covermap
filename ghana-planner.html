Honest by design. The headline is coverage — envenomings within reach of the right antivenom — what the tool controls. Deaths are a decision-gap anchored to a directly observed Ghanaian measurement: case-fatality among treated carpet-viper patients rose 1.8% → 12.1% when an ineffective product was substituted (Visser 2008). It is what the product choice governs — not extra lives versus today, since effective antivenom already reaches some patients. Subnational stock is unobservable, so this models a placement choice, not measured inventory. ~5.6% of burden sits beyond 50 km of any hospital. Robust: 24/25 hospitals stay chosen even if the gradient is flattened.
PANAF-Premium is the recommended product: WHO risk-benefit-assessed, broad sub-Saharan cover, and heat-stable (lyophilised, no refrigeration) — deployable in rural facilities without cold chain. Facility frame: patients counted have already reached care, so no care-seeking multiplier is applied (an earlier version double-counted it).
